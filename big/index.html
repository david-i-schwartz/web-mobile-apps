BIG: Brick Image Generator

Turn a picture into a brick mosaic on real LEGO® baseplates and plates, flat or raised in relief. Choose the parts, join studs into bigger pieces, and get a parts list. Everything stays in this browser.

Brick placement

  • Inspect shows a stud’s colour, part and code, height and place.
  • Mark placed ticks off the whole part a stud belongs to, so you can follow your build.
  • Edit paints colours, part styles or heights onto studs. Drag to paint a line. Undo is in the edit bar.
  • On a keyboard, the arrow keys move between studs, and Space or Enter acts on the selected one.

Sizing

  • − and + zoom out and in, up to 12 times closer.
  • Fit shows the whole mosaic again.
  • Zoomed in, drag or scroll inside the board to move around. In Edit, dragging paints, so switch to Inspect to move.
  • Focus on section, under the board once you tap a stud, zooms to that stud’s 16 × 16 section.

Views

  • Colours is the mosaic as you’ll build it.
  • Heights is a grey map of how high each stud stands: lighter is higher. It needs Relief (step 9).
  • 3D shows the mosaic from an angle, with raised parts standing up.
  • Codes labels every part with its code, like paint by numbers. RED22T is a Red 2 × 2 tile.
  • Compare, under the board, slides your picture over the mosaic from the left to check the match. It only changes the view.

Mosaic

  • Work down the steps. Each change re-makes the mosaic straight away, and your edits are laid back over it.
  • Simple, bold pictures with clear light and dark areas make the best mosaics. Crop close to the subject.
  • A mosaic looks best from a few steps back: each stud is one 8 mm pixel.

Choose or paste a picture on the board to begin.

1. Crop

  • The crop box keeps the shape of your sheets. Drag inside it to move it, or drag a corner to zoom in or out.
  • Dashed lines show where one sheet ends and the next begins.
  • Auto-trim edges cuts off flat borders, like a plain background around a logo.

2. Sheets

  • A mosaic is built on standard LEGO sheets laid side by side. Pick the sheet, then how many across and down.
  • Baseplates are thin and flexible; studs only. Plate 16 × 16 is rigid. The LEGO Art canvas is the 16 × 16 Technic brick that LEGO Art sets join with pins.
  • Each stud is 8 mm, so a 48 × 48 baseplate makes a mosaic 38.4 cm across.
  • Mosaics are split into 16 × 16 sections, the size LEGO Art builds in, so you can work one section at a time.

3. Background

  • Leave background empty clears the plain area around the subject, starting from the picture’s edges. Empty studs show the bare sheet and need no parts.
  • Tolerance sets how different a colour can be and still count as background.
  • Pick background colour lets you tap the colour to clear in the crop picture.

5. Colours

  • These are 41 solid colours from LEGO’s current range, under their BrickLink names. Tap a colour to leave it out or bring it back.

6. Matching and dithering

  • Colour match decides which LEGO colour is closest. CIE76 and CIEDE2000 judge colours the way eyes do; Oklab is a newer, simpler formula; RGB is the plainest.
  • Dithering mixes two colours in a speckle to fake one LEGO doesn’t make. It helps smooth skin and skies, but adds grain and 1 × 1 parts.
  • Merge rare folds colours used on only a few studs into their nearest neighbour.

8. Pieces

  • Plates have studs on top. Tiles are smooth. Round plates and round tiles are one stud each and give a dotted look. All four are the same height, so they mix freely.
  • Choose a style for the whole mosaic, change it colour by colour, or paint it onto areas with the Part style tool in Edit.
  • Biggest first covers each patch of one colour with the largest part that fits. Rows uses only 1-wide parts along each row. Studs only uses one part per stud, like LEGO Art.
  • A part only joins studs of the same colour, style and height. Round parts are always 1 × 1.
  • Keep parts inside sections means each 16 × 16 section builds on its own.
  • Not every part is made in every colour. BrickLink’s wanted list shows which ones sellers have.

  • Greedy algorithm, Wikipedia: Biggest first works this way. It is quick, but not always the fewest parts possible.

9. Relief

  • Relief raises parts on hidden plates, one plate (3.2 mm) per step, so areas of the picture stand out from the sheet.
  • From light areas raises the brightest parts most; From dark areas does the opposite; Paint only starts flat and you raise areas with the Height tool in Edit.
  • Hidden plates stack under the raised parts and are joined into bigger plates too. Nobody sees them, so pick a cheap colour.
  • Look at the result in the Heights or 3D view above the mosaic.

About this version 1.0 · 13 Nov 2026
  • A new app for brick mosaics, grown from an early LEGO mode for FIG. (For its first day it was called LIG; anything saved then still opens.)
  • 14 Nov: paste boxes for pictures, one picture button at a time, the Tilted view renamed 3D, and Heights and 3D explain themselves and can turn relief on. Later that day: a Codes view and numbered map, with a code for every kind of part (RED22T is a Red 2 × 2 tile) in the parts list, lists and wanted list. Then printable build sheets: a PDF with real-size pages for every section, and FIG’s Compare slider.
  • Mosaics are built on real sheets: baseplates 48 × 48, 32 × 32 and 16 × 32, Plate 16 × 16, or the LEGO Art canvas, as many across and down as you like.
  • Plates, tiles, round plates and round tiles, chosen for the whole mosaic, per colour, or painted on.
  • Three ways to join studs into parts, with the part count for each, and 16 × 16 sections.
  • Relief: parts raised up to four plates on hidden plates, from the picture’s light or dark areas or painted by hand, with Heights and 3D views.
  • A parts list with spares, a BrickLink wanted list, project files, progress marking and editing with undo.
