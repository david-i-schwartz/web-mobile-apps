FIG: Fusebead Image Generator

Turn an image into a bead pattern on square, hex or iso pegboards. Isolate a colour, tap beads off as you place them, and work one chunk at a time. Progress is saved in this browser. You can also edit the pattern by hand and keep several patterns.

This browser can’t save right now (its storage is full or switched off). You can keep working, but changes made from now on won’t be remembered after you close the page.

Bead Placement

  • Inspect is for looking. Tap a bead to see its colour, coordinate and chunk under the board. Nothing gets ticked off, and you can mark, clear or focus on that bead’s whole chunk from there.
  • Mark placed is for building. Tap a bead to tick it off as placed, and tap it again to undo.
  • Edit is for changing the pattern by hand. It opens the paint tools above the board and the Edit panel.
  • Keyboard: the arrow keys move the selection. Space ticks the selected bead, or uses the edit tool on it in Edit.

Sizing

  • − and + zoom out and in, up to 12 times closer.
  • Fit shows the whole board again.
  • Zoomed in, drag or scroll inside the board to move around. In Edit with “Drag paints on a touch screen” ticked, dragging paints instead, so switch to Inspect to move.
  • Focus on this chunk, under the board once you tap a bead in Inspect, zooms to that chunk.

Views

  • Compare, under the board, slides your picture over the pattern from the left, so you can check the match. It only changes the view.
  • Your edits sit on top of the converted pattern. The picture is still converted underneath, exactly as before.
  • Paint puts the paint colour on a peg. With a mouse or pen you can drag to paint a line.
  • Erase empties a peg.
  • Fill recolours the whole patch of touching pegs that share one colour. On an empty peg it fills the whole empty area around it.
  • Pick takes the colour of the bead you tap as the paint colour, then switches to Paint.
  • Undo and Redo step through every edit, including swaps, slides and resizes. Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y do the same. The history lasts until you close the page.
  • On a touch screen a tap edits one peg and a drag still scrolls the board. Tick Drag paints on a touch screen, in the Edit panel, to paint with a drag.
  • A peg whose bead changes loses its placed tick.

Swap or Drop a Colour

Changes one bead colour everywhere.

  • Choose the colour to change in the list. Each entry shows how many beads use it.
  • Swap for the paint colour shows every bead of that colour in the paint colour instead.
  • Drop it takes the colour out. Each of its beads goes to the next-closest colour already in the pattern, judged from the picture, the way Merge rare does it.
  • Both are rules, so they still hold after you change a setting. Each rule is listed here and can be taken back.

    Slide and Resize

    Moves the whole pattern, or changes the board under it.

    • The arrows slide the whole pattern along the board’s own directions: four on square boards, six on hex and iso.
    • Beads pushed past the edge are kept. Slide back and they return.
    • The middle button centres the pattern on the board.
    • Board size adds or trims pegs without re-making the pattern: whole boards on square, a ring on hex, a column or row on iso.
    • New pegs appear on the right and at the bottom, or all round on hex. Slide the pattern to where you want it.
    • Use these to move a board seam off a face, or to fit a pattern that is one row too tall.

    Board size

    Pattern

    A pattern is one bead colour, or no bead, for every peg on a board. The board sets how the pegs are laid out and how each peg is named.

    • Square: pegs in straight rows and columns, on 29 × 29 boards joined edge to edge. Each bead touches four others. A picture’s pixels are laid out the same way, so this suits pixel art and most pictures.
    • Hex: one six-sided board. Every second line of pegs is shifted by half a peg, so each bead touches six others, and curves and slanted edges look smoother. Each peg is named by three numbers, q, r and s, counted from the centre.
    • Iso: the same shifted layout on a rectangular board, such as a perforated sheet. Its three directions sit 60° apart, which is what lets cubes and other 3D-looking designs line up. Each peg is named by its column and row.
    • Chunks split a board into small named areas, so you can build one area at a time.

    Learn More

    • Hexagonal grids, Red Blob Games: every way to number a hex layout, including q, r, s and shifted rows, with pictures you can play with.
    • Grid parts and relationships, Red Blob Games: square, hex and triangle grids side by side.

    1. Crop

    • Drag a corner to resize the box, or drag inside it to move it.
    • Auto-trim edges strips flat-coloured margins such as white borders. Busy edges, like a screenshot’s status bar, still need a manual drag.
    • Reset crop brings back the whole picture.

    2. Size

    • Fit shows the whole crop. The width slider sets its width in beads, the height follows the crop, and boards are added around it automatically. Leftover pegs stay empty.
    • Fill covers every peg. Width moves in whole boards and you choose how many boards down. The crop box locks to that shape, so moving it decides what gets cut.
    • Ticks on the slider mark whole boards. Tap a number to jump there.
    • Snap to whole boards makes the slider settle on a whole board when you stop within two beads of one.
    • Boards are 29 × 29 pegs, numbered column-row from the top left.
    • Chunks are 10 × 10 areas of a board: A to C across, 1 to 3 down.

    • Fit keeps the whole crop on the board. Image size shrinks it, and the pegs around it stay empty.
    • Fill covers every peg. The crop box locks to the board’s shape, so moving it decides what gets cut. On a hex board the picture’s corners fall off the board.
    • Board side is the number of pegs along one edge of the hexagon. Point up and Flat edge up turn the board. Coordinates are q, r and s, counted from the centre peg, and they always add up to zero. Chunks are the six wedges, numbered clockwise from the top.
    • Offset every second column or row shifts it by half a peg, the same layout as the Iso bead designer. Columns and rows are numbered from the top left. Chunks are 10 × 10 blocks: A, B, C… across and 1, 2, 3… down.

    3. Background

    • Leave background empty empties the colour touching the picture’s edges and anything connected to it. Enclosed areas of that colour, like white lettering, stay.
    • Tolerance sets how close a colour must be to count as background. Raise it if specks of background remain; lower it if it eats into the picture.
    • Pick background colour is for a background that isn’t the colour at the picture’s edge. Tap that colour in the crop picture. The patch you tap is emptied, along with any of that colour touching the edge.
    • Use edge colour goes back to the automatic choice.

    Learn More

    • Flood fill, Wikipedia: the method used here, which spreads from a starting point through matching neighbours.

    4. Adjust Picture

    Shifts the picture’s colours before beads are matched.

    Use these when the pattern comes out too dark, too flat or too grey.

    • Brightness lightens or darkens every colour.
    • Contrast pulls light and dark apart, which helps a subject stand out.
    • Saturation makes colours stronger or weaker. At its lowest the picture turns grey.
    • Hue turns every colour around the colour wheel.
    • Warmth leans the picture towards orange or towards blue.
    • Your picture file is never changed, and the background is found before these are applied.

    5. Colours

    How Each Bead Samples the Picture

    • Photo (average) blends all the pixels under a bead. Best for photos.
    • Pixel art (exact) takes the single pixel at the bead’s centre. Best when one pixel should become one bead.
    • Dominant colour takes the most common colour under the bead, which keeps cartoon and logo edges crisp.
    • Median takes the pixel of middle brightness, so a few stray pixels can’t tint the bead.

    Where the Bead Colours Come From

    • Basic 16 is a starter palette you can edit. Untick a colour to leave it out, change its colour or name, or add your own.
    • Catalog picks the closest real beads from your bead sheet (.xlsx or .csv), so the shopping list works. Until a sheet is imported, the Basic 16 colours are used.
    • Scheme recolours the picture in one family: greyscale, a rainbow, or shades of a single colour from dark to light.

    Catalog Choices

    • Max colours limits how many different beads the pattern uses. Fewer colours means fewer bags to buy and an easier build. The app drops the colours that matter least and gives their beads to the next-closest colour.
    • Brands: untick a brand to shop from fewer brands.
    • Translucent, glow and mixes are off by default because their screen colours aren’t reliable.
    • The shopping list counts bags at 1,000 beads each.

    Scheme Choices

    • Shades sets how many steps the scheme has.
    • By brightness ignores the picture’s own colours. Dark areas get the darkest shade and light areas the lightest, with the contrast stretched so every shade is used.
    • By colour picks the closest shade to each original colour, which suits Rainbow.
    • Add black and white puts a black and a white at the ends of the scheme.
    • Use real beads from my sheet swaps each shade for the closest real bead, and the shopping list works as usual.

    Learn More

    • Color difference, Wikipedia: the ways “closest colour” can be measured, from plain RGB distance to CIEDE2000.
    • A perceptual color space for image processing, Björn Ottosson: a readable account of what makes a colour space match what we see.

    6. Dithering and Matching

    Dithering

    • Dithering fakes in-between shades by mixing nearby bead colours. It adds single-bead speckles, so it suits photos about 50 beads wide or more.
    • None gives flat areas of colour. Best for pixel art and logos.
    • Diffuse is Floyd–Steinberg error diffusion: each bead’s colour error is passed on to the beads after it. On hex and iso boards it spreads to the neighbouring pegs.
    • Atkinson passes on only three quarters of the error and spreads it further: over two rings of pegs on hex and iso boards. Flat areas stay cleaner and contrast is higher.
    • Ordered adds a regular 4 × 4 pattern instead of passing errors on.
    • Strength softens or strengthens the dithering.

    Colour Match

    • This is how the closest bead is judged.
    • RGB is quick but crude.
    • CIE76 measures distance in a colour space built to match how we see differences.
    • CIEDE2000 refines that, mostly for blues, greys and strong colours.
    • Oklab is a newer colour space of the same kind. It often handles blues and purples better than CIE76, so try each on your picture.
    • When Max colours trims the catalog, the colours to keep are chosen with CIE76. Every bead is then matched with the formula you pick.

    Merge Rare

    • Merge rare removes any colour used on fewer pegs than the number you set, and gives those beads to their next-closest colour already in the pattern.

    Learn More

    • Image dithering: eleven algorithms and source code, Tanner Helland: error diffusion step by step, including Floyd–Steinberg and Atkinson.
    • Ditherpunk, Surma: ordered and other dithering, with pictures.
    • Color difference, Wikipedia: CIE76 and CIEDE2000.
    • A perceptual color space for image processing, Björn Ottosson: the article that introduced Oklab.

    Colour match

    7. Clean Up

    Tidies the pattern after matching.

    • Remove specks gives any stray patch of that many beads or fewer to the colour that touches it most.
    • Smooth lets each bead take the colour that more than half of its neighbours share. It clears speckle and thin stray lines, and each extra pass smooths more.
    • Outline draws a one-bead line in your darkest colour around the subject, where it meets the emptied background. Inside recolours the subject’s edge beads; Outside adds beads around it. It needs empty pegs inside the picture, so turn on Leave background empty or use a see-through picture.
    • Dithering adds specks on purpose, so cleaning up a dithered pattern undoes some of it.
    • Merge rare runs after Remove specks and Smooth.

    Learn More

    Project File

    • Save project file writes one .fig.json file. It holds the picture, your settings, your edits, your placed ticks, and the finished pattern bead for bead.
    • Open project file brings a pattern back exactly. If this browser would convert the picture a little differently, the file’s own record of the pattern is used.
    • If the pattern uses real beads from your sheet, the sheet travels in the file and replaces the sheet loaded here.
    • Library keeps several patterns in this browser, each with a small picture. Saving under a name already in the list replaces that pattern.
    • The library lives in this browser only, and clearing the browser’s site data removes it. Save a project file for anything you want to keep.
