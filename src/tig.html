TIG: Tile Image Generator

Turn a picture into a tile mosaic on mesh sheets, then follow it tile by tile. Everything stays in this browser.

Tile Placement

  • Inspect shows a tile’s colour and place.
  • Mark placed ticks a tile off, so you can follow your build. Tap it again to untick it.
  • Edit paints colours onto tiles. Drag to paint a line. Undo is in the edit bar.
  • On a keyboard, the arrow keys move between tiles, and Space or Enter acts on the selected one.

Sizing

  • − and + zoom out and in, up to 12 times closer. On a computer, Ctrl and the mouse wheel zoom too.
  • Fit shows the whole grid again.
  • Zoomed in, drag or scroll inside the grid to move around. In Edit, dragging paints, so switch to Inspect to move.

Views

  • Compare, under the grid, slides your picture over the tiles from the left, so you can check the match. It only changes the view.

Picture

This section turns your picture into tiles: each tile takes the colour of the part of the picture it covers, like a pixel. Each step below changes how that happens, and the grid updates as you go. Your picture never leaves this browser.

Choose or paste a picture on the grid to begin.

1. Crop

Drag the box to move it and its corners to resize it. The box keeps the shape of your boards. Auto-trim cuts away flat borders.

2. Size

The grid is made of whole boards. Choose a board, then how many go across and down.

3. Background

Leave background empty clears the colour round the picture’s edges, so those tiles stay empty. Raise the tolerance to clear more. If the background isn’t at the edges, pick its colour in the crop picture.

4. Adjust

5. Colours

Tap a colour to leave it out or put it back. Max colours keeps only the most useful ones.

6. Matching and Dithering

  • Match by: how “closest colour” is measured. RGB is quick; CIE76, CIEDE2000 and Oklab follow how eyes see colour.
  • Sample by: how one tile’s colour is taken from the pixels under it. Centre pixel suits pixel art.
  • Dithering mixes colours in patterns to suggest shades the palette lacks.
  • Merge rare gives colours used fewer times than this to their nearest neighbour.
