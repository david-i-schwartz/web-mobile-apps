    How the coordinates work

    A peg is two whole numbers. On a square board, column and row times 5 mm give its position. On this board one column right moves 4.33 mm across and 2.5 mm down, so every second column sits half a peg lower and all three cube edges become straight lines of pegs.

    Charts count from 1 because that’s how you count pegs. The maths uses axial coordinates: q steps along the slanted axis, r steps straight down. Sliding a design is just adding to q or r. The third cube coordinate s = −q − r keeps all three summing to 0.

    A cube at (X, Y, Z) lands at (X − Y, Y − Z). Add 1 to all three and you land in the same place, so an isometric view loses depth. Cubes are drawn far to near in order of X + Y + Z, so nearer faces cover farther ones.

    Each face becomes k × k beads: a bead takes the colour of the triangle just down-right of its peg, so every face gets exactly the same count.
